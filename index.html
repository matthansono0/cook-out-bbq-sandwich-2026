The Cook Out BBQ Sandwich: How to Order It Like a Regular

By Matt Hanson

Cook Out is famous for its trays, its burgers, and its 40-plus milkshake flavors, but the BBQ sandwich is the quiet favorite on the menu. It does not get the attention the Big Double burger gets, and it does not have a fan club like the Cajun chicken sandwich, but for a lot of regulars in the Southeast, the BBQ sandwich is the order they come back for. Here is how to order it, what to pair it with, and how to get the best value out of it.

First, what is it? The Cook Out BBQ sandwich is a chopped pork barbecue sandwich, served the Eastern North Carolina way that the chain grew up with. It is simple, smoky, and a little tangy, and it comes on a soft bun that holds up well to the juicy pork. It is not a fussy sandwich. It is the kind of food that tastes like a cookout, which is exactly the point of the place.

The smartest way to order it is as a tray. A Cook Out tray comes with your main, two sides, and a drink, and a regular tray usually runs about $6.39 to $6.99 depending on your location. That is the best value on the menu, and it turns a single sandwich into a full meal. You can also order the BBQ sandwich on its own if you only want a snack, but the tray is what most regulars choose, and it is the better deal by a clear margin.

Picking the two sides is half the fun, and it is where a lot of first-timers go wrong. The classic pairing with barbecue is slaw and hushpuppies. The slaw cools the sandwich down, and the hushpuppies add the crispy, cornmeal crunch that barbecue needs. If you want something heavier, fries are the safe pick, and Cajun fries add a little heat that works well with the smoky pork. Onion rings are the other strong choice, and they hold up better than you might expect on a drive home. Quesadillas are also on the tray sides list at many locations, and they are an underrated pick if you want something different from the usual fries.

For the drink, Cheerwine is the regional answer. It is a cherry soda from North Carolina, and it has been the default Cook Out drink for years. Sweet tea is the other classic. If you want to finish sweet, you can swap the drink for a fancy milkshake on a tray, and the upgrade costs $1.00 to $1.60 depending on your location. A vanilla or banana pudding shake after a BBQ tray is a lot of food, so save that move for a day when you are properly hungry.

A few practical notes help. Prices vary by location, and your local menu board has the final word, so treat any price you see online as a guide rather than a promise. Cook Out is a late-night chain across the Southeast, and many locations stay open well past midnight, which is part of why the BBQ sandwich has such a loyal following after evening events. And if you are feeding a group, trays are the easy answer: everyone picks a main and two sides, and nobody has to agree on one thing.

The BBQ sandwich is not the flashiest item on the Cook Out menu, but that is why regulars love it. It is consistent, it is affordable, and it tastes like the region it comes from. Order it as a tray, pick slaw and hushpuppies, get a Cheerwine, and you will understand why it has lasted on the menu for years.
